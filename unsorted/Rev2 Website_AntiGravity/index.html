The Broken Transition

The on-ramp closed.
And it closed unevenly.

Shop class disappeared from most schools between the 1990s and 2010s as standardized testing requirements crowded the timetable. At the same time, consumer products stopped being openable.

The result is students arriving at college engineering programs having never used a screwdriver or touched a circuit board — not because they are less capable, but because our educational system removed the entry point.

And it was removed unevenly: it still exists for families who can buy expensive maker kits or afford private robotics teams. MicroLab restores that critical on-ramp for everyone.

The Pedagogy

The Breakthrough Moment

A student sits over a breadboard. For the first hour, she follows the wiring diagram step by step, unsure whether this subject belongs to her.

Then comes the challenge: change the code so the display prints something of her own choosing. She navigates the MicroPython string function, types mom is cool onto the OLED screen, and uploads it.

The screen lights up. She jumps out of her seat and calls her mother over across the room to look. That's the exact moment engineering stops being someone else's discipline.

The Parent Investment Guide

Is a computer science degree still worth it?

An honest look at recent engineering labor data, the real dropout risks, and how MicroLab de-risks your family's college investment.

~6%

Recent CS Grad Unemployment

Higher than the average college graduate (~5%) due to generalist entry-level saturation. However, underemployment remains among the lowest and starting median pay remains the highest.

Embedded

Where the Demand Shifted

Engineers who understand physical hardware, sensors, firmware, and power distribution are in high demand across automotive, defense, and healthcare.

The Real Risk: 40%+ Switch Majors After Spending $40,000+

Computer Science and Engineering carry one of the highest attrition rates in higher education. University research consistently identifies the root cause: students arrive with an inaccurate mental picture of what the field actually is.

42% of university STEM entrants transfer out or drop out prior to junior year (National Center for Education Statistics).

The De-Risking Offer: Both Outcomes Are Wins

A low-cost, hands-on test before thousands of dollars in tuition are committed.

Your student discovers an authentic appetite for embedded programming, circuits, and problem-solving. They enter college with a two-year practical head start and a working GitHub portfolio that sets them apart for internships.

Outcome B: The Student Discovers It's Not for Them

Your student realizes they prefer a different discipline. You just saved your family $20,000 to $60,000 in misdirected college tuition and prevented two years of academic frustration.

# MicroPython Live Process Loop (Running on Core 1)
from machine import Pin, ADC, time_pulse_us
import time

# Pin Definitions
led_alert = Pin(18, Pin.OUT)
led_night = Pin(2, Pin.OUT)
led_heartbeat = Pin(19, Pin.OUT)
adc_light = ADC(Pin(34))
adc_light.atten(ADC.ATTN_11DB)

trig = Pin(5, Pin.OUT)
echo = Pin(18, Pin.IN)

print("[BOOT] Industrial supervisor thread started.")

while True:
    # 1. Heartbeat toggle
    led_heartbeat.value(not led_heartbeat.value())

    # 2. Sample 12-bit ADC
    raw_adc = adc_light.read()
    light_pct = (raw_adc / 4095.0) * 100
    if light_pct < 30.0:
        led_night.value(1) # Engage auxiliary lighting
    else:
        led_night.value(0)

    # 3. Read ultrasonic time-of-flight
    # Distance < 20 cm triggers active emergency brake
    time.sleep(1.0)

The Origin & The Method

Building the Engineers Who Keep Physical Systems Running

👨‍🏫

Gary Khan

Founder & Lead Engineer

20+ Years in Engineering, Microcontroller Architecture & Educational Design

"I founded MicroLab because I saw first-hand the chasm opening between theoretical classroom instruction and physical reality. Students were graduating high school having memorized calculus formulas and dragged blocks around code scratchpads, but had never held a multimeter, debugged a ground loop, or felt an inrush current brownout."

"When you write software for a web server and it fails, you get an error message. When you write firmware for a motor and it fails, the motor draws stall current and burns the trace off your PCB. That consequence is what makes embedded systems deeply engaging and durably valuable."

"MicroLab is dedicated to giving every young person the entry point into that layer — because our physical world depends on it."

Rooted in the Community

Based in Cincinnati, Ohio, MicroLab operates directly within local Community Learning Centers, YMCAs, libraries, and public community spaces. We believe that physical engineering cannot be learned in isolation through a screen — it requires hands-on mentoring, physical tools, and collaborative lab benches.
