The Problem

A ball is thrown vertically upward from a platform. Its height above the ground, in metres, after t seconds is modelled by the quadratic function:
h(t) = −4.9t² + 15t + 2
where 15 m/s is the initial upward velocity and 2 m is the initial height of the platform.

Use the simulation and algebra to answer the following questions:

  1. How many seconds after being thrown does the ball hit the ground? Round to two decimal places.
  2. What is the maximum height reached by the ball? Round to two decimal places.
  3. How many seconds after being thrown does the ball reach its maximum height? Round to two decimal places.

Worked Solution

1

Identify the quadratic equation

The height function is h(t) = −4.9t² + 15t + 2.

Compare this to the standard form at² + bt + c = 0:

a = −4.9, b = 15, c = 2.

2

Find when the ball hits the ground

At ground level, h(t) = 0. So we solve:

−4.9t² + 15t + 2 = 0

Use the quadratic formula: t = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

Substitute the values:

t = (−15 ± √(15² − 4 × −4.9 × 2)) / (2 × −4.9)

t = (−15 ± 16.50) / −9.8

This gives two solutions. One is negative (before the throw) so we ignore it. The positive solution is:

Ground time ≈ 3.19 seconds

3

Find the time at maximum height

For a parabola, the vertex occurs at t = −b / 2a.

t = −15 / (2 × −4.9) = 1.53 s

Maximum height occurs at 1.53 seconds

4

Find the maximum height

Substitute the vertex time back into the height equation:

h(1.53) = −4.9(1.53)² + 15(1.53) + 2

Maximum height ≈ 13.48 metres

5

What the graph shows

The curve is a downward-opening parabola. The roots (where it crosses the t-axis) tell us when the ball is at ground level. The vertex is the highest point. The part of the curve above the t-axis represents the time the ball is in the air.
