How does a robotic car work?

A microcontroller (ESP32) sends electrical signals to a motor driver (DRV8833), which powers two DC motors. By changing the speed of each motor independently, the car can go forward, backward, and turn! A buck converter steps down the battery voltage to safely power the ESP32.

For Teachers & Students

This simulator shows how an ESP32 microcontroller drives two DC motors through a DRV8833 motor driver. Key ideas:

  • ESP32 GPIO pins send PWM (pulse-width modulation) signals to the DRV8833 control pins. PWM lets us vary motor speed, not just on/off.
  • DRV8833 is a motor driver — the ESP32 can't power motors directly. Each motor needs 2 pins: one for direction (forward/reverse) and one for speed (PWM).
  • Differential steering: To turn left, slow down the left motor or run it in reverse while the right motor goes forward. Try the sliders!
  • Buck converter: Batteries give 6V but the ESP32 needs 3.3V. The buck converter safely steps down the voltage.
  • TT motors: These are cheap, simple DC motors. Reverse the wires (or the signals) to make them spin the other way.
